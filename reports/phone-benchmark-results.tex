Primary study:\allowbreak{} 99 successful fresh-process runs, comprising 45 latency runs, 45 inference-memory runs and nine accelerated replay runs. Latency and memory each have five processes per fitted candidate. The replay study has one process per candidate and is functional validation only.

\section{Definitions and abbreviations}
This reference explains the terms used in the result tables. A statistic must be read together with its units, sample count and table caption:\allowbreak{} the observations may be inference calls, process medians, memory snapshots or fitted candidates.

\midrule\endfirsthead
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\small\itshape Continued from previous page}\\
\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Definition and interpretation} \\
\midrule\endhead
\midrule\multicolumn{2}{r}{\small Continued on next page}\\\endfoot
\bottomrule\endlastfoot
N /\allowbreak{} count & Number of observations used for a statistic. Each per-model warm-latency table has N=5,000 calls from five processes; the between-process-median table has N=5. A larger N does not by itself mean more independent models or participants. \\
Mean & Arithmetic average:\allowbreak{} sum the observations and divide by N. Unusually slow calls can pull the mean upward. \\
Median /\allowbreak{} p50 & Middle value of the ordered observations, averaging the two middle values when N is even. Approximately half the observations lie at or below the median. \\
p95 & 95th percentile. Approximately 95\% of observations are at or below this value and 5\% are higher. For latency it describes the slower end of the measured calls. Ties, interpolation and rounding affect the exact proportion. \\
SD /\allowbreak{} std & Sample standard deviation:\allowbreak{} a measure of spread around the mean, in the same units as the observations. This report uses denominator N-1. A larger SD means more dispersion; it is not the standard error or a confidence interval. \\
N=1, SD=0 & For a single observation, sample SD cannot be estimated. The exporter prints zero by convention; this does not demonstrate that the operation has no variability. \\
Per-model vs window p95 & Per-model latency p95 is calculated across 5,000 measured calls. Window-summary p95 is calculated across the three seed candidates' summary values. These percentiles describe different sets of observations. \\
Confidence interval (CI) & An interval produced by a statistical method to quantify uncertainty in an estimated quantity. A p95 is a distribution percentile, not a 95\% confidence interval. This benchmark report does not calculate confidence intervals. \\
--- in a table & No value is available or the metric does not apply. A dash does not represent zero. \\
\end{longtable}\endgroup
Example:\allowbreak{} a latency p95 of 0.224 ms means approximately 95\% of measured calls finished within 0.224 ms. It does not promise that every call finishes within this time. For 5,000 calls, the slowest 5\% corresponds to about 250 calls.

The exporter uses linear interpolation at zero-based position 0.95(N-1). Equivalently, order values from x(1) to x(N) using one-based ranks. With h=1+0.95(N-1), j=floor(h), and g=h-j, the percentile is:\allowbreak{}

\[\operatorname{p95}=(1-g)x_{(j)}+g x_{(\min(j+1,N))}.\]
For N=5,000, h=4,750.05, so the result is 95\% of the 4,750th ordered value plus 5\% of the 4,751st. For three candidate summaries, h=2.9, so their p95 is 10\% of the middle value plus 90\% of the largest. Neither calculation is a maximum or a confidence bound.

\begingroup\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}

\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Definition and interpretation} \\
\midrule\endhead
\midrule\multicolumn{2}{r}{\small Continued on next page}\\\endfoot
\bottomrule\endlastfoot
RAM & Random-access memory:\allowbreak{} the device's working memory. The device RAM figure is distinct from memory attributed to this app. \\
PSS & Proportional Set Size. Counts memory resident for the process, assigning shared pages in proportion to the number of processes sharing them. It is useful for attributing physical-memory cost without charging every process the full shared page. \\
RSS & Resident Set Size. Counts the process's resident memory, including shared pages at their full size. Summing RSS across processes can count the same shared page more than once. \\
Native PSS & Android's native-heap PSS category, read from Debug.MemoryInfo.nativePss. It is one part of total PSS, not a measurement of every native allocation or all native-library code pages. \\
Dalvik PSS & Android's managed-runtime heap PSS category, read from Debug.MemoryInfo.dalvikPss. The historical name remains in the API even on Android's ART runtime. It does not represent all Flutter/\allowbreak{}Dart memory. \\
Baseline & Snapshot after initializing the global runtime and loading the prepared input/\allowbreak{}expected fixtures, with no model session loaded. It already includes substantial benchmark-harness memory. \\
Loaded & Snapshot after model asset reading and inference-session creation, before the first prediction and validation calls. \\
Warmed & Snapshot after parity validation and, for inference runs, 100 explicit warmup calls. Replay warms the model through validation before playback. \\
Observed /\allowbreak{} end & Observed snapshots are taken during inference or replay. End snapshots are taken after that workload; replay end is recorded for each session. These phase labels do not imply continuous memory tracing. \\
Delta /\allowbreak{} incremental PSS & A phase snapshot minus the same process's baseline. Deltas are calculated within each process before aggregation. They estimate extra process cost and include runtime/\allowbreak{}validation/\allowbreak{}harness effects, not only model weights. \\
Observed peak PSS & Largest PSS value among the collected snapshots. A brief higher allocation between snapshots may be missed. \\
High-water RSS & Largest RSS reached during the process lifetime so far, as reported by the operating system. It includes earlier initialization and validation and is not a per-phase incremental peak. \\
\end{longtable}\endgroup
Illustrative example:\allowbreak{} if a process has 4 MiB of private resident memory and shares another 10 MiB equally with one other process, its RSS is 14 MiB and its PSS is 4+10/\allowbreak{}2=9 MiB. Separately, a hypothetical baseline PSS of 120 MiB and warmed PSS of 150 MiB give a within-process delta of 30 MiB. These examples are explanatory, not additional phone measurements.

\noindent Memory terminology and category names follow the \href{https://developer.android.com/tools/dumpsys\#meminfo}{Android memory inspection documentation} and the \href{https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/Debug.MemoryInfo}{Debug.MemoryInfo API reference}.\par

Replay processing & Buffer work since the preceding window, quality checks/\allowbreak{}feature extraction and the full inference call. Decode and waiting are recorded separately or excluded, as specified in the report. \\
Schedule lag & How late a replay prediction finishes relative to its window's expected completion time. It includes processing and scheduling delays; it is distinct from native inference latency. \\
Window W /\allowbreak{} step & W is the duration of sensor history used to compute features. Step is the interval between successive window starts. Here W=100/\allowbreak{}120/\allowbreak{}140 seconds and step=50/\allowbreak{}60/\allowbreak{}70 seconds. \\
Observation delay & Time required to collect a full valid sensor window before the first result. It is separate from millisecond-scale processing and may be longer when quality checks reject a window. \\
Seed /\allowbreak{} candidate & A seed controls random choices in tuning/\allowbreak{}training. A candidate is one fitted window/\allowbreak{}seed model. These candidates have different winning hyperparameters, so seed comparisons do not isolate randomness alone. \\
Protocol complete & The report's completeness flag:\allowbreak{} at least five fresh release processes, recorded code versions and no recorded thermal interruptions, plus the required call counts or memory phases. It is not an accuracy guarantee or a claim of population representativeness. \\
